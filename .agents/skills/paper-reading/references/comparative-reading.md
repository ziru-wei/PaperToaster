# Comparative Reading

Search every supplied paper file for the comparison axes in the current question. Read contiguous supporting passages from every paper before making a cross-paper claim. Search headings or related terms when a direct term is absent. If a relevant section is absent from a file, say so rather than assuming its contents.

Build the answer around the comparison axes that actually resolve the user's question. Do not write separate summaries per paper followed by a brief comparison at the end. After identifying differences, evaluate whether they reflect a meaningful underlying distinction or mainly surface-level variation.

**Honor explicit priorities and exclusions in the question.** If the question targets method, focus on method; if it asks beyond method, shift to contribution, problem framing, assumptions, explanatory mechanism, empirical target, evidence, or implications. Do not privilege contribution-level analysis by default.

**Select comparison axes dynamically based on the question**, rather than applying a fixed set. Possible axes include:
- problem boundary and motivating gap
- conceptual or theoretical claim
- mechanism, system, or intervention
- empirical target and evaluation evidence
- resulting capability, finding, or artifact
- limitations and scope of the claimed advance

**When the question challenges whether two papers, approaches, or claims are meaningfully different**, test the distinction rather than merely articulating each source's framing. Ask whether the apparent difference remains after abstracting away incidental differences such as domain, terminology, implementation, scale, or presentation. Do not assume that different domains, artifacts, techniques, or author framings imply substantively independent contributions.

**Separate source-stated differences from comparative inference.** Support cross-paper conclusions with evidence from every paper involved. For inferred distinctions, make the evidential basis and confidence clear; do not turn a useful framing into an established fact simply because it creates a clean comparison.

**Do not force symmetry.** The evidence may show that one work is a broader framework, a domain-specific extension, a stronger implementation, a partial overlap, or only weakly distinct from another. State that directly when appropriate.

Prioritize relevant file passages rather than summarizing all content. Cite each paper's source-span IDs only where the cited passage supports the claim.
